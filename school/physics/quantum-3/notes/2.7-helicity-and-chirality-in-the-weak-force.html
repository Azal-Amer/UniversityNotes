So why did this even happen? What was different between the electro and the pion?
This interaction should be supressed by the weak interaction, because the regular particle is right handed!

Our particle going the opposite direction, our spins hasve to be balanced. The opposite particles have to carry the spin the other way. The directions on the momentum and the direction on the spin match each other, and we know from last time matching diorections means it's right handed.[1]

This happens because chirality is NOT helicity. The weak interaction is connected to the chirality.

Chirality is the same exactly as helicity for massless particles, but the more massive the more chirality can be different than the helically. Because the muon is more massive than the electron, it's helicity (which comes from arrow direction) is more distinct from the chirality. The electron's interaction becomes suppressed.

  • In electrons chirality is almost like helicity since it's lightweight
  • In muons chirality it's not much at all like helicity since it's heavy
